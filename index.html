Learn the slide rule.

This page rebuilds the old browser-captured slide-rule archive as a real, hands-on rule. Drag the slide and the cursor, read the printed scales yourself, and see why logarithms make multiplication work.

Big idea

The slide rule replaces multiplication with addition of distances on a logarithmic scale.

What you will learn

How to align the scales, read a product off the printed numbers, and keep track of the decimal point like a pro.

How to think about it

A slide rule uses two matching log scales. When you move one scale so that a number lines up with 1 on the other scale, every other number on the moving scale now represents a factor. That is why the tool can do multiplication and division so fast.

  • Fix one number. Align it with the 1 on the other scale.
  • Read the other factor. The answer is under the second number.
  • Track the decimal. The rule gives you the digits; you supply the place value.

The original archive

If you are brand new to slide rules, start dragging the demo above, then come back here to read the short scale guide below.

1. The D scale

The D scale is the main working scale, printed on the fixed top and bottom bars. It never moves — only the slide and the cursor do.

2. The C scale

The C scale is printed on the slide. You line up its 1 with a number on D, then read the result under a second number.

3. The cursor

The cursor is the transparent hairline window that helps you see where the answer lands as the scales move. It is the third reference point, not a third numbered scale.

Practice moves

  1. Multiply: line up 1 on C with one factor on D, then find the second factor on C and read the product on D under it.
  2. Divide: reverse the idea and use the scales to compare a known product with a divisor.
  3. Estimate first: the slide rule is fast because it is excellent at approximation, then you refine the decimal place mentally.

Tip: if the answer extends past the visible decade, a real slide rule simply carries the decimal place into the next decade.

Common scales to learn

C and D

The workhorse pair for multiplication and division. These are the first scales to master.

 A and B

Two-decade versions of C and D, useful for squares and square roots.

 K

The cube scale. It handles cubes and cube roots on many classic rules.

 CI and DI

Inverse scales. Handy when the number you need to line up is inconvenient on the normal scale.

 L

A logarithm scale used for direct log readings on some rules and examples.

 S and T

Trig scales for sine, tangent, and related angle work on rules with trig features.
