Slide Rule Topic 2

Learn the A and B scales.

A and B are the two-decade cousins of C and D. They are ideal for squares and square roots, and they show how the same slide-rule logic can stretch across a wider range.

Hub C and D A and B K CI and DI L S and T
Core idea

Two decades in one scale.

A and B usually span 1 to 100, split across two decades. That means you can square a number by lining it up on A and reading the answer on B, or take a square root by reversing the process.

  • A pairs with B the way C pairs with D.
  • The extra range makes square roots feel natural.
  • Keep track of the decade, because the visible number may be only part of the answer.
Practice

Try squaring a number

1. Pick 2 or 3 on the A scale.
2. Find the matching spot on B.
3. Read the squared value and decide the decimal place.

Once that feels comfortable, try square roots by starting with the answer on B and tracing back to A.

Why it matters

The same trick, stretched farther.

The A/B pair helps learners see that a slide rule is not just for multiplication. It is a logarithmic map for several related operations.

Next step

Ready for cubes?

After squares and square roots, the K scale is the natural next stop. It extends the same idea to cubes and cube roots.
